# The cut list - what is on screen, block by block (issue #23).
#
# The live take didn't survive contact: shot 1 printed "outside trading window"
# because we recorded after the close, and only the three browser steps ever made
# it to disk. So the video is assembled from parts instead of performed in one
# pass - slides rendered from slides.pdf, terminal shots rendered by vhs, the
# browser footage cut from what we already have, narration recorded separately.
#
# assemble.py reads this file. Picture is cut to fit the narration, never the
# other way round: each row's length is its narration block's audio length times
# `share`, so re-recording one line and re-rendering is the whole edit loop.
#
#   row    | kind  | source        | in    | out   | narr | share | note
#
# kind:
#   slide  a slide of ../slides.pdf, named by a distinctive piece of its title
#          rather than its number, and resolved through SLIDES.md. #244 inserted
#          a slide at 8 and moved Results from 18 to 19 while this branch was
#          open; SLIDES.md and README-export.md both index by number and nothing
#          guards either, so this one does not index by number. A title that no
#          longer matches exactly one slide is an error, not a wrong picture.
#   shot   source is build/shots/<name>.mp4, rendered by tapes/make.sh (vhs)
#   clip   source is footage/<name>.<mp4|mov|...> - screen recordings
#
# in/out are seconds into the source; `-` means the whole thing. A clip is
# speed-fitted to its target length within 0.5x-2.0x, then frozen on its last
# frame if it is still short. Slides are stills, so they simply hold.
#
# narr names both the file (../video/narration/<narr>.<mp3|m4a|...>) and the
# block, matched against its heading in narration.md - by name, because #247
# inserted a block at 8 and positional ids would have quietly shifted every take
# after it onto the wrong block. Two rows may share
# one block (11 does: the digest, then the deploy history under its tail); their
# shares are fractions of that block and must sum to 1.
#
# `self` in the share column means the clip was narrated live and carries its own
# sound. Such a row still names its block - the heading is what --stamp writes
# the real offsets back into - but takes its audio from the clip. Those
# rows play at native speed and set their own length from where the talking
# starts and stops - audio and picture are one recording, and speed-fitting a
# talking screen recording to a separate voice file desynchronises the words
# from the thing they are pointing at.
#
# `secs` fallbacks live in narration.md's own headings and are used only when no
# audio for that block exists yet - that is what --scratch and a silent rough
# cut run on.
